Health claim rejected for pre-existing disease: what the rules say

Rules checked on 16 Oct 2026. Sources are listed at the end.

Short answer

A pre-existing disease is one diagnosed or treated in the 36 months before your policy began. If you declared it, its waiting period cannot be longer than 36 months of continuous cover. After 60 months of continuous cover, the insurer cannot reject a claim for non-disclosure unless it proves fraud. Write to the insurer's grievance officer first, then go to the Insurance Ombudsman.

What counts as a pre-existing disease

Under IRDAI's definition, a pre-existing disease is a condition, illness or injury that a doctor diagnosed, or for which medical advice or treatment was recommended, within the 36 months before your policy started. An illness that ended more than 36 months before the policy began does not fit this definition.

Check three things first

  1. How long you have been continuously insured. Count from your first policy, including any policy you ported from another insurer. Continuous means renewed every year without a break.
  2. Whether you told the insurer about the illness in the proposal form when you bought the policy.
  3. What proof the insurer relies on. Rejections are sometimes based on one line in the hospital file, such as a duty doctor's note. Ask for the dated record that shows the illness began before the policy.

What the rules say about your situation

Your situationWhat the rules say
You declared the illness and have 36 months or more of continuous coverThe waiting period for a declared pre-existing disease cannot be longer than 36 months. It is over, so this reason should not apply.
You have 60 months or more of continuous coverThe insurer cannot contest the claim for non-disclosure or misrepresentation unless fraud is established. The 60-month rule.
Under 60 months, and you did not declare an illness you knew aboutThis is the weakest position. The insurer can dispute the claim, but it still has to show the illness meets the definition above.

These limits come from the IRDAI rules that took effect on 1 April 2024 for new and renewed policies.

What to do next

  1. Write to the insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer. Quote the rule that applies, and ask for the evidence and the exact policy clause. Insurers must acknowledge a grievance at once and resolve it within 14 days.
  2. If there is no fix in 14 days, you can also complain to IRDAI on the Bima Bharosa portal (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in), by email to complaints@irdai.gov.in, or on 155255.
  3. Go to the Insurance Ombudsman once the insurer rejects your complaint, or one month after you wrote with no reply. It is free and handles claims up to ₹50 lakh. File within one year. How to file.
  4. The Consumer Commission is the next step if needed. A complaint must be filed within two years of the cause of action, usually the rejection.
