Health claim rejected after 5 years of cover? The 60-month rule

Rules checked on 16 Oct 2026. Sources are listed at the end.

Short answer

After 60 months (5 years) of continuous health cover, an insurer cannot contest your policy or your claim on the ground that you did not disclose something or gave wrong information, unless it establishes fraud. Years with an earlier insurer count if you ported. Permanent exclusions written in the policy still apply.

The rule in IRDAI's words

"After completion of sixty continuous months of coverage (including portability and migration) in a health insurance policy, no policy and claim shall be contestable by the insurer on grounds of non-disclosure, misrepresentation, except on grounds of established fraud. This period of sixty continuous months is called as moratorium period."

IRDAI repeated this in its Master Circular on Health Insurance Business dated 29 May 2024. Before 1 April 2024 the moratorium period was 96 months (8 years).

What it covers

  • Rejections that say "non-disclosure", "misrepresentation" or "concealment of material facts" about your health.
  • Years with an earlier insurer, if you moved through portability, and moves between plans of the same insurer (migration).

What it does not cover

  • Fraud that the insurer establishes.
  • Permanent exclusions written in your policy contract.
  • A gap in cover. Continuous means renewed without a break, so check your renewal dates.

How to use it

  1. Find your first policy start date. It is on your oldest policy schedule or your portability papers.
  2. Count the months from that date to the date of admission.
  3. If it is 60 or more, write to the insurer's grievance officer, quote the rule above, and ask whether the insurer alleges fraud and on what evidence.
  4. If the insurer does not change its decision, go to the Insurance Ombudsman. It is free.
