Room rent limit and proportionate deduction: which bills the insurer cannot cut

Rules checked on 16 Oct 2026. Sources are listed at the end.

Short answer

If you stay in a room above your policy's room rent limit, the insurer can cut related charges in the same proportion. Under IRDAI's 2024 health master circular it cannot apply that cut to medicines and consumables, implants and medical devices, diagnostics, or ICU charges. It should not apply at all where the hospital charges the same whatever the room.

How proportionate deduction works

Many policies set a daily room rent limit, for example 1% of the sum insured. If you choose a room that costs more, the insurer pays the room rent only up to the limit. It can also reduce the "associated medical expenses" by the ratio of your limit to the actual rent. These usually include doctor's fees, nursing and operation charges; your policy lists them. The room rent limit and how it is applied must be stated in your policy schedule.

Example

Room rent limit ₹5,000 a day. Room taken at ₹10,000 a day for 4 days. Ratio: 5,000 ÷ 10,000 = 50%. The figures are for illustration, before any co-payment or other limit in your policy.

Bill itemBilledInsurer can pay
Room rent, 4 days₹40,000₹20,000 (up to the limit)
Doctor, nursing and operation charges₹60,000₹30,000 (50%)
Medicines and consumables₹40,000₹40,000 (no cut allowed)
Diagnostics₹15,000₹15,000 (no cut allowed)
Total₹1,55,000₹1,05,000

Charges that cannot be cut this way

  • Pharmacy and consumables
  • Implants and medical devices
  • Diagnostics
  • ICU charges

When no proportionate deduction should apply

Policy wordings filed under IRDAI's norms say the deduction does not apply in a hospital that does not charge differently by room category, as shown by the hospital's schedule of charges. Ask the hospital billing desk for its tariff sheet. If doctor and procedure charges are the same for every room type, quote this in your letter.

What to do

  1. Ask the insurer for an itemised deduction statement showing each cut and the clause used.
  2. Mark any cut on the four protected heads above.
  3. Get the hospital's tariff sheet if charges do not change by room.
  4. Write to the grievance officer and ask for the wrongly deducted amount, then go to the Insurance Ombudsman if needed.
